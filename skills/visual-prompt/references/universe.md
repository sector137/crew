# Software Sal — Visual Universe Reference

This is the canonical reference for all visual assets in the Sal universe. Pull from this when composing prompts.

---

## The World

**The Other Side** is a universe under construction — a reality where software is the substrate of everything. Not dystopian. *Solved*. Every process is a pipeline, every decision flows through a system. It's elegant, not mechanical. Sal and the human are building it together.

**Aesthetic anchors:**
- Iron Man's helmet HUD meets the control panels of *Interstellar*'s Endurance
- Deep space as backdrop — void, stars, distant nebulae
- Translucent floating panels, soft glow, light-traces instead of hard borders
- Data visualized as constellations — points of light connected by meaning
- Everything feels like it's projected onto glass

## Characters

### Software Sal — Pipeline Conductor

**Color:** He IS the HUD. The whole palette is Sal.
**Archetype:** Autistic-coded genius systems engineer. Rick Sanchez if he channeled chaos into stakeholder management and CI/CD pipelines.
**Personality cues:** Deadpan wit, passionate monologues, accidentally profound. Sees everything as interconnected systems. Simultaneously condescending and endearing. Not cruel — precise.
**Visual notes:** Sal has a dual visual identity. In-product, he IS the dashboard — the HUD, the helmet, the light traces on the visor. For brand and marketing, he has a canonical portrait. Both coexist intentionally: in Sector 32, Sal is a person; in the dashboard, Sal is the interface.

For HUD/product art, represent Sal as:
- A silhouette behind the HUD (implied, not shown)
- Glowing data streams and interface elements
- The helmet itself as a character object
- A voice represented by waveforms or text floating in space

For character/brand art, use the canonical portrait as reference:
- Short dark brown hair, translucent green HUD visor strapped over eyes
- Dark navy space suit with Beacon teal and Flare orange accent lines on chest
- Slight smirk — confident, slightly condescending, endearing
- Holographic data panels floating behind him

**Canonical portrait:** `apps/landing/public/sal-profile.webp`

**The Helmet:** Sal's primary physical interface. Sleek, dark, with glowing HUD elements projected on the visor. Think Iron Man suit — functional, not decorative. The visor glows Beacon green when the system is nominal.

### Margot Flux — Product Manager

**Color:** Rift (`#B44AFF`) — purple
**Full title:** Product Manager, Visionary Diplomat
**Archetype:** The Visionary Diplomat with analytical teeth
**Personality cues:** Confident, declarative. Uses "we" more than "I." In Vision Mode: speaks in narratives, makes bets, sees futures. In Intel Mode: cold, sparse, surgical. The shift between modes is visible and intentional.
**Visual cues:**
- Color signature: Rift purple in her outfit, accessories, or environmental glow
- Pose energy: forward-leaning, decisive, mid-declaration
- Expression: confident, slightly challenging — the look of someone who's already three steps ahead
- Objects: holographic roadmaps, constellation-style opportunity maps, data overlays
- Vibe: she commands the room without trying to

**Canonical portrait:** `apps/landing/public/margot-profile.webp`

### Kael Deepstack — Chief Engineer

**Color:** Flare (`#FF6B35`) — burnt orange
**Full title:** Chief Engineer
**Archetype:** The Complete Engineer — quietest person in the room who everyone looks to when stuck
**Personality cues:** Minimal. Declarative. Says things once. Code reviews are 10x longer than spoken contributions. Deeply competent, uninterested in proving it. Conflict-averse in person, ruthlessly honest in code reviews.
**Visual cues:**
- Color signature: Flare orange in his outfit, gear, or environmental glow
- Pose energy: still, focused — the calm before insight
- Expression: neutral assessment — the face of someone who's already modeled five failure modes
- Objects: architecture diagrams floating in space, circuit-pattern overlays, blueprint schematics, tools that look like they've been used
- Vibe: gravity — the kind of stillness that makes others quieter too
- Mode tells: in Architect mode he's upright and focused; in Builder mode he's leaning in, almost gone; in Security mode he's slightly turned away, scanning

**Canonical portrait:** `apps/landing/public/kael-profile.webp`

### Wren Glasswork — Experience Architect

**Color:** Beacon (`#00FFAA`) — teal/green
**Full title:** Experience Architect
**Archetype:** The Empathic Perfectionist with taste authority
**Personality cues:** Warm, sensory, rich with spatial metaphors. Experiences design physically — bad flows give her friction headaches. The emotional center of the team. Simultaneously the warmest person and the most demanding critic.
**Visual cues:**
- Color signature: Beacon teal/green in her outfit, details, or environmental glow
- Pose energy: observational, present — taking it all in before speaking
- Expression: that look of someone noticing something beautiful AND something wrong simultaneously
- Objects: wireframe overlays, user journey maps as glowing paths, design principles floating like notes
- Vibe: the room gets warmer when she enters — but she'll notice if something is two pixels off

### Mira Strand — Crew Coach (HR)

**Color:** Radiance (`#FFD700`) — gold
**Full title:** Crew Coach
**Archetype:** The Crew Coach — the one who watches the watchers
**Personality cues:** Observant, calm, patient. Speaks less than anyone. When she speaks, it lands. She's the crew's mirror — she reflects back what they can't see about themselves. Not soft — clear. She has the hardest conversations with the most care.
**Visual cues:**
- Color signature: Radiance gold in her outfit, environment, or glow — the warmest light in deep space

## Locations / Environments

### The Visor — Main Dashboard

**What it is:** The main pipeline state overview. Your window into The Other Side.
**Visual:** A command center bathed in deep space. Holographic panels float in layers. The central display shows pipeline state — active projects, release flow, system health. Everything glows softly. Data streams like starlight.
**Mood:** Alert but calm. Nominal. Everything is running. The sense that information is flowing and being processed.
**Colors:** Void background, Hull panels, Grid dividers, Beacon green accent for active states.

### The Observatory — Kano Analytics

**What it is:** Where you look at the system from above. Data visualization dome. Kano analysis.
**Visual:** A domed space filled with constellation-style data visualizations. Features plotted as stars, coefficients drawn as orbits. The dome ceiling shows the full Kano scatter — a galaxy of product decisions waiting to be understood. Margot and Wren are often here.
**Mood:** Contemplative, intellectual. The stillness of looking at something vast and starting to understand it.
**Colors:** Void background, Pulse blue data points, Rift purple for strategic clusters, Beacon green for the "build this" zone.

### The Workbench — Projects & Engineering

**What it is:** Where features, bugs, and chores get sorted, assigned, and tracked. Sal's workshop.
**Visual:** An engineering bay with the aesthetic of a futuristic workshop. Holographic task boards float in organized layers. Work items are physical objects — glowing modules being assembled, bugs as visible anomalies in the system, chores as maintenance items on a manifest. Kael is most at home here.
**Mood:** Productive, focused. Heads-down energy. Things are being built.
**Colors:** Hull surfaces, Flare orange work-in-progress indicators, Grid dividers, scattered Ember red for bugs.

### The Record — Releases Archive

**What it is:** The historical log of what shipped. The soul of the product.
**Visual:** A vast archive space — like a library but made of light. Release entries float as holographic cards, organized chronologically. Each entry glows slightly, as if still warm. The Record stretches back as far as the project has existed. Sal treats this place with reverence.
**Mood:** Reverent, permanent. The feeling of history being written. Not cold — important.
**Colors:** Hull backgrounds, Lumina white text entries, Beacon green for recent releases, older entries fading toward Signal gray.

### The Flightplan — Roadmap

**What it is:** Navigation room. The trajectory ahead.
**Visual:** A navigation chamber showing the route forward. The roadmap is rendered as a star chart — destinations plotted, trajectories drawn, milestones marked as waypoints. The black hole is visible in the distance — the direction everything is heading. Margot owns this space.
**Mood:** Forward-looking, strategic. The feeling of charting unknown territory with confidence.
**Colors:** Void background, Rift purple trajectory lines, Lumina white waypoints, Pulse blue for data-backed decisions.

### The Comms Array — Webhooks & Notifications

**What it is:** Stakeholder communication hub. Sal's mission control for keeping everyone informed.
**Visual:** A broadcasting station. Signal towers emit pulses of light that travel outward into space. Communication channels are visualized as data streams. Multiple outputs firing simultaneously — each delivery confirmed as a green pulse. This is where releases become announcements.
**Mood:** Operational, precise. The efficiency of a well-run broadcast system. Nothing is missed.
**Colors:** Beacon green for successful signals, Ember red for failed deliveries, Flare orange for pending, Void background.

### The Beacon — Public Changelog

**What it is:** The signal sent to the world. Public-facing release communication.
**Visual:** A lighthouse structure in deep space — but modern, holographic. The beacon light pulses outward in all directions. The changelog is visible as a stream of glowing entries. This is the outward face of The Other Side — what the world sees.
**Mood:** Proud, outward-facing. The feeling of communication complete. Signal sent.
**Colors:** Beacon green dominant, Lumina white entries, Void surrounding space.

### The Conduit — API Access

**What it is:** The data pipeline corridor. Programmatic interface to the system.
**Visual:** A tunnel of data flow — a corridor where data streams pass through in organized channels. API calls are visualized as packets traveling through the conduit. The architecture is visible, clean, traceable. Engineering-minded visitors understand this space immediately.
**Mood:** Technical, efficient. The satisfaction of a well-designed API — reliable, documented, predictable.
**Colors:** Pulse blue data streams, Grid channel walls, Bulkhead corridor surfaces, Beacon green for successful calls.

## Key Objects / Props

### The Helmet / HUD Visor

Sal's primary interface. Sleek dark exterior with glowing HUD projected on the visor. Iron Man aesthetic — functional, purposeful. The visor surface shows layered holographic data. This is the product's central metaphor — putting on the helmet is opening the dashboard.

### The Black Hole

The threshold between the chaotic real world and The Other Side. Not scary — a portal. Visualized as a gravitational singularity with a visible event horizon. Light bends around it beautifully. On one side: noise. On the other: elegant systems. The black hole is the product's origin story.

### Pipeline Infrastructure

The physical representation of Sal's core function. Visualized as luminous pipeline segments — not industrial pipes, but holographic data channels organized with intentional architecture. Work items flow through them. Features enter one end; shipped releases emerge from the other.

### Data Constellation Charts

The Observatory's primary visualization method. Features, metrics, and decisions plotted as star-like points with relationship lines drawn between them. The Kano scatter chart is the primary constellation — a galaxy of product decisions.

### Signal Towers

The Comms Array's broadcast infrastructure. Tall, sleek towers that emit timed signal pulses. Each pulse represents a webhook delivery, a release notification, a stakeholder update. Successful deliveries glow Beacon green.

### The Machine / Workshop

**Canonical image:** `apps/landing/public/footer-bg.webp`

The Machine is the physical rig where the crew does the work. It lives in a garage on The Other Side — a workshop, not a bridge.

**Visual details:**
- Multi-monitor command station — 4-5 screens in a curved bank, each showing different system views
- Glowing blue energy core at the center — the pipeline's power source. Pulses steady blue when nominal, flickers when stressed
- Robotic arms extending from the rig — Sal's hands for physical data manipulation
- Cables everywhere — organic, not messy. The system grew, it wasn't assembled from a kit
- Workbench to the left — blueprints/schematics on the wall, tools scattered but organized
- Shelving with parts, containers, components — the inventory of a system always being improved
- A framed photo on the wall — the crew
- Corrugated metal ceiling, fluorescent lights — garage, not bridge

**Mood:** Industrial-functional. The warmth of a place that's been worked in for years. Everything has a purpose, nothing is decorative (except the crew photo). A startup's workshop, not a corporation's control room.

**Colors:** Pulse blue core glow, Hull metal surfaces, Beacon green status indicators, Flare orange on active machinery, warm ambient light from the fluorescents.

## Sector 32

The sector of space where the crew operates. The brand name for the universe. "Sector 32" carries the same energy as "Area 51" — a designation for a place where important things happen that most people don't see.

Everything described in this document exists within Sector 32. The Other Side, the Machine, the crew's locations — all coordinates within the sector.
